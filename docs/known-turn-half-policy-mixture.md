# Prospective half mixture of two saved turn policies

**Issue #4; November 25, 2026 (PST).** The fully qualified [PR #116 result](known-turn-collision-medium-transfer-result.md) found that an exact-turn policy improved three of four matched 40m pairs, but all four candidate exploitabilities exceeded the fixed `0.01` initial-pot limit and wet A regressed. [PR #117](https://github.com/jimmy-sim/poker-ai/pull/117) stopped at source qualification and produced no row result. This distinct strength question asks whether a fixed equal **behavior-policy mixture** of PR #116's two saved final policies improves exact restricted-game decisions on those same four **known, outcome-inspected** inputs. A behavior-policy mixture averages legal-action probabilities at each exact information row; it is not a new training path or a root-level random choice between complete policies.

## Fixed strength decision

The primary comparator is the **q8 control on each same game**, independent of which parent previously won. A positive result requires all four mixed final values `<=0.01` initial pots, each also `<=0.025` as a redundant worst check, and at least three mixed-minus-q8 differences `<=-0.001` pots including one dry and one wet, with the fourth `<=+0.001`. Any absolute failure or any paired difference `>+0.001` is **negative**. Otherwise the result is **inconclusive**. These gates preserve PR #116's final-value and paired scale. Report exact-turn parent comparisons descriptively; neither a favorable parent choice nor one favorable input can replace the fixed q8 comparator. No default promotion follows even from a positive result without a separate generalization protocol.

This mixes two policies trained on the **same** four selected games, so it measures known-input policy combination only. A local mixture's best response may differ from either parent's; improvement cannot be inferred by interpolating their already known scores. There is no test of unseen inputs, sampling error, human play or full-game strength.
