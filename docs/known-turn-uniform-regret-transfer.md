## Question and why it matters

[PR #119's published diagnostic](known-mixture-residual-map-result.md) identifies a 0.708274-chip turn best-response gain on known dry B after 50:50 mixing, while [PR #116's exact-turn candidate](known-turn-collision-medium-transfer-result.md) still misses the absolute strength gate on all four known medium OOP games at 40m charged work. [PR #114](known-turn-work-budget-result.md) found that extra work alone did not meet its wet absolute gate; [PR #112's regret-recency update](known-turn-regret-recency-result.md) regressed on its wet game. These failures remain unchanged. A *different, already implemented* weighting rule may change the difficult turn decisions without changing information, legal actions or work.

The earlier [140-cell controlled-learning study](controlled-learning-comparison.md) measured a full expected, signed-regret, exact-representation path on the **different already known** `dev_medium_oop` game at 10m work. With linear strategy averaging held fixed, uniform regret time weight scored `0.17970431725763525%` of initial pot versus `0.2890218798613533%` for linear regret time weight. These are [pinned raw rows](../results/controlled-learning/report/checkpoints.csv), not a seed interval, evidence on the four games below, or a guarantee of transfer. This prospective study asks: **does that single uniform-regret weighting choice improve exact-turn play at matched work across all four PR #116 games?** The previous experiment included other rule combinations; none will be screened or substituted here based on the new outcomes.
